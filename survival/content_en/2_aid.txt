@@ aid|aid_first|🆘|First things first|Safety – Check – Call
1. SAFETY: Is it safe to approach? (fire, electricity, traffic, collapse). If not, do not become a victim too.
2. RESPONDING? Tap the shoulders gently and ask loudly “Are you OK?”.
3. BREATHING? Tilt the head back, lift the chin, look-listen-feel for 10 seconds.
4. CALL 112 or 166. Put the phone on speaker and do what they tell you.
5. Not breathing normally → CPR right away (“CPR” button).
6. Breathing but not responding → Recovery position (on the side).
7. Heavy bleeding → Press on it right away.
## What to say to 112 / 166
- WHERE you are (address or coordinates from the “Compass”).
- WHAT happened. HOW MANY injured. Whether they are BREATHING.
- Your phone number. DO NOT hang up first.

@@ aid|panic|🫁|Panic attack – psychological first aid|For someone else and for yourself
+ In disasters most people are not injured in the body – they are hurt in the mind. A calm person beside them is the first aid.
## How to recognise a panic attack
- Sudden, very intense fear. Pounding heart, breathlessness, “I can’t get enough air”, chest tightness, dizziness, numbness in hands or lips, trembling, sweating, “I’m going to die” or “I’m going crazy”.
- It peaks within about 10 minutes and then eases. It is very frightening but is NOT dangerous in itself.
! It may be the HEART and not panic: first time, over 40 years old, pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back, cold sweat, not passing within 15–20 minutes, known heart disease or asthma. If in DOUBT: 166 / 112. Better an unnecessary call.
## Helping someone having a panic attack
1. Stay calm. Panic is “contagious” – and so is calm. Speak slowly and softly.
2. Take them to a quieter place, away from crowds and noise. Get down to their level.
3. Say simply: “I am here. You are safe. This is a panic attack, it will pass in a few minutes. Breathe with me.”
4. Breathe with them: IN through the nose counting 4 … OUT through the mouth slowly counting 6. The long EXHALE calms the heart. You count out loud.
5. Grounding 5-4-3-2-1: “Tell me 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste.” It brings the mind back to the present.
6. Give them something to hold: a cool bottle of water, a wet towel on the face, feet firmly on the floor.
7. Stay with them until it passes. Then water and rest.
- DO NOT say “calm down”, “it’s nothing”, “stop it”. It does not help and causes shame.
- DO NOT put a paper bag over their mouth. It is outdated advice and can cause harm – especially if it turns out to be the heart or asthma.
## If it happens to me
1. Tell yourself: “This is panic. I have had it before or I know about it. It will pass. Nothing will happen to me.”
2. Sit down or lean against a wall. Feet on the floor.
3. Breathe 4 in – 6 out, for 2–3 minutes. Or “box breathing”: 4 in, hold 4, 4 out, hold 4.
4. Grounding 5-4-3-2-1 on your own, or count objects around you.
5. Do not run away if you are safe – the wave passes faster when you do not fight it.
## Psychological first aid after a disaster
- LOOK: is the area safe? Who is injured, who is in shock, who is alone?
- LISTEN: approach, introduce yourself, ask what they need. Listen without pressing them to talk. Silent presence beside them is also help.
- LINK: water, blanket, food, a phone to find their family, accurate information. Do not promise what you do not know (“they will surely be found”).
- Give them a small task if they can: holding the flashlight, handing out water. They feel in control of something again.
- Crying, anger, numbness, sleeplessness, nightmares in the first days are NORMAL after something frightening.
## Children
- Keep them TOGETHER with their parents. Hugs, holding hands, close by.
- Explain simply and truthfully: “There was an earthquake. Now we are safe. The grown-ups are taking care of things.”
- Let them play, draw, ask the same questions again and again – that is how they “digest” it.
- Turn off the TV showing images of the disaster. Keep routine as much as possible: meals, sleep, bedtime story.
## When a specialist is needed
- If after 2–4 weeks someone cannot function, cannot sleep, keeps reliving the event, or talks about death or that life is “not worth living”.
- 10306: National Psychosocial Support Line – free, anonymous, 24 hours a day (also for children and families).
- 1018: Suicide Prevention Helpline (KLIMAKA), 24 hours.
- 1056: The Smile of the Child – helpline for children, 24 hours.
! If someone is at risk of harming themselves NOW: do not leave them alone and call 112.

@@ aid|cpr|❤️|Adult CPR|Hard and fast in the centre of the chest
1. Lay the person on their back on a hard surface.
2. Call 112/166. If there is a defibrillator (AED), shout for someone to bring it.
3. Put the heel of your hand on the CENTRE of the chest, the other hand on top, arms straight.
4. Push HARD 5–6 cm deep, FAST 100–120 times a minute. Press the ♪ button for the rhythm.
5. If you know how: 30 compressions – 2 rescue breaths. If you don't know or don't want to: compressions ONLY, without stopping.
6. As soon as a defibrillator arrives: switch it on and do what it says out loud.
7. Do not stop until the EKAB ambulance (166) arrives, the person starts breathing, or you are exhausted. Swap every 2 minutes if there are two of you.
! Doing CPR wrong is better than doing nothing. You cannot make them worse.
## Child (1 year – puberty)
- One or two hands, depth ~5 cm (1/3 of the chest). If you are alone, do 1 minute of CPR before you leave to call.
## Baby (under 1 year)
- Two fingers in the centre of the chest, just below the nipples, depth ~4 cm, 100–120 a minute.
+ Take a Basic Life Support (BLS) course – a few hours, it could save someone you love.

@@ aid|choke|😮|Choking|Something stuck in the throat
## Adult / child who is coughing
- Encourage them to cough hard. Do not hit yet.
## Cannot cough, speak or breathe
1. 5 back blows: lean them forward, hit hard between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
2. 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich): from behind, fist above the belly button, other hand on top, pull sharply in and up.
3. Keep going 5 + 5 until it comes out.
4. If they become unconscious: lay them down, call 112, start CPR.
## Baby
- Face down along your forearm, head low: 5 back blows. Then on the back: 5 chest thrusts with 2 fingers. NO abdominal thrusts on a baby.
## Are you alone?
- Give yourself abdominal thrusts with your fist, or push hard against the back of a chair.

@@ aid|bleed|🩸|Heavy bleeding|PRESS – PRESS – PRESS
1. Put on gloves if you have them (or a plastic bag).
2. Press HARD on the wound with a clean cloth/gauze. Use all your weight.
3. Do not lift the cloth to look. If it soaks through, put more on top.
4. Tie a bandage tightly over the cloth.
5. Lay them down, cover them (shock). Call 166.
## Blood spurting or not stopping on an arm/leg → Tourniquet
1. Put the tourniquet 5–7 cm ABOVE the wound (not on a joint).
2. Tighten by turning the rod until the bleeding stops. It hurts – that is normal.
3. Write down the TIME (on the forehead or on the tourniquet).
4. DO NOT loosen it. Only the hospital removes it.
+ Improvised: wide cloth (4 cm+) and a stick/spoon to twist. No wire or thin cord.
## Wound on the body / neck / armpit / groin
- Pack the wound with gauze (packing) and press hard for at least 3 minutes. Do not pull out objects stuck in the body – hold them in place.
## Nosebleed
- Sit, lean FORWARD (not back), pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes.

@@ aid|shock|😰|Shock – fainting|Lay down, keep warm, call
- Signs: pale, cold, sweaty skin, fast weak pulse, dizziness, thirst, confusion.
1. Stop any bleeding.
2. Lay them on their back. If the legs/pelvis are not injured, raise the legs a little.
3. Cover them (underneath too). Talk to them calmly.
4. Nothing to eat or drink.
5. Call 166.
## Fainting
- Lie down, legs up. If not recovered in 1 minute → 166 and recovery position.

@@ aid|recovery|🛌|Recovery position|Breathing but not responding
1. Kneel beside them. Place their arm nearest you at a right angle, palm up.
2. Their other arm: back of the hand against their cheek.
3. Bend the far knee and pull on the knee to roll them onto their side towards you.
4. Tilt the head back to keep the airway open, mouth facing down (so vomit can drain).
5. Keep checking their breathing. If it stops → CPR.
! If you suspect a spinal injury (fall, car crash), do not move them unless needed for them to breathe or to save them.

@@ aid|burn|🔥|Burns|Cool water for 20 minutes
1. Move away from the fire/source. Clothes on fire: put them out.
2. Cool running water (not ice-cold, no ice) for 20 minutes.
3. Remove rings, watch, clothing (if not stuck to the skin).
4. Cover loosely with clear cling film or a clean plastic bag.
5. Call 166 if: large burn, face, hands, genitals, child/elderly person, electrical or chemical.
! NO butter, toothpaste, oil, yoghurt. DO NOT pop blisters.
## Chemical burn
- Rinse with plenty of water for at least 20 minutes. Remove contaminated clothing.
## Electric shock
- Turn off the power FIRST. Do not touch them while they are in contact. Then check breathing → 166.

@@ aid|fracture|🦴|Fracture – sprain|Keep it still
1. Do not try to straighten the bone.
2. Keep it still as it is: splint from cardboard, a magazine, a branch, tied above and below the fracture.
3. Arm: sling from a triangular cloth or a T-shirt.
4. Ice wrapped in a cloth for 20 minutes.
5. Open fracture (bone showing): cover with clean gauze, press around it (not on it), 166.
## Sprain (twisted joint)
- Rest, ice 20 min, elastic bandage, raise it.
## Head injury
- Vomiting, drowsiness, confusion, unequal pupils, fluid from nose/ears → 166 at once. Do not leave them alone for 24 hours.

@@ aid|wound|🩹|Cuts – wounds|Clean, cover, watch for infection
1. Wash your hands, put on gloves.
2. Rinse the wound with plenty of clean (drinking) water for several minutes. Remove dirt.
3. Antiseptic around the wound, no alcohol inside it.
4. Cover with sterile gauze or a dressing. Change it every day.
- Signs of infection: spreading redness, warmth, pus, fever, red streaks → doctor.
+ Is your tetanus vaccine up to date? Booster every 10 years.

@@ aid|allergy|💉|Severe allergy (anaphylaxis)|Adrenaline at once
- Signs: swelling of face/tongue, trouble breathing, wheezing, hives all over the body, dizziness.
1. If they have an adrenaline auto-injector (EpiPen etc.): into the outer thigh, even through clothing.
2. Call 166 – even if they get better.
3. Trouble breathing: let them sit up. Dizzy: lie down, legs up.
4. If no better in 5–15 minutes and there is a second injector, use it.

@@ aid|heart|🫀|Heart attack – Stroke|Every minute counts
## Heart attack
- Chest pain/tightness spreading to the arm, jaw, back, with sweating, nausea, shortness of breath.
1. 166 at once.
2. Sit them down to rest.
3. If not allergic and not forbidden by a doctor: 1 aspirin 300 mg to chew.
## Stroke – FAST test
- FACE: Smile – does one side droop?
- ARMS: Raise both arms – does one drift down?
- SPEECH: Say a sentence – are the words mixed up?
- Any one of these = 166 NOW. Note the time it started.

@@ aid|seizure|⚡|Seizures – Epilepsy|Protect, do not hold down
1. Move hard objects away. Something soft under the head.
2. DO NOT hold them down. DO NOT put anything in the mouth.
3. Time it. Over 5 minutes → 166.
4. When it stops: recovery position, stay with them.

@@ aid|diabetes|🍬|Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)|Sugar fast
- Diabetic who is sweating, shaking, confused: give 15–20 g sugar (juice, 4 sugar cubes, sweets).
- If no better in 15 minutes, give again. If they become unconscious: nothing by mouth, recovery position, 166.

@@ aid|poison|🧪|Poisoning|Poison Centre 210 7793777
1. Call the Poison Centre 210 7793777 (24 hours) or 166.
2. Keep the product packaging.
3. DO NOT make them vomit. DO NOT give milk or oil unless told to.
4. On skin/eyes: rinse with water for 15–20 minutes.
## Carbon monoxide (CO)
- Headache, dizziness, nausea in several people in the same house, drowsiness → FRESH AIR at once, get everyone out, 166.

@@ aid|bite|🐍|Bites – stings|Snake, scorpion, bee, tick
## Viper
1. Stay calm. Keep the limb still, low at heart level.
2. Remove rings, watches (it will swell).
3. Go to hospital/166. Note the time.
! NO cutting, NO sucking, NO tourniquet, NO ice.
## Bee – wasp
- Scrape the sting out with a fingernail or a card. Cold compress. Watch for allergy if face/throat swells.
## Tick
- Tweezers very close to the skin, pull straight out without twisting. Note the date. Fever or red ring in the following weeks → doctor.
## Sea urchin – weever fish
- Weever fish/stingray: soak in hot water (as hot as you can bear, ~45 °C) for 30–90 minutes – the pain eases.

@@ aid|eye|👁️|Eye|Rinse, do not rub
- Dust/chemical: rinse with clean water for at least 15 minutes, from the nose side outwards.
- Object stuck in the eye: do not remove it, cover BOTH eyes (so they don't move), 166.

@@ aid|dehyd|💧|Dehydration – diarrhoea|Fluids with salt and sugar
- Signs: thirst, dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness. In children: few wet nappies, sunken eyes.
- Rehydration drink (if you have no pharmacy sachets): 1 litre clean water + 6 teaspoons sugar + 1/2 teaspoon salt. Sip by sip.
! Babies, elderly people, blood in the stool, high fever → doctor.
