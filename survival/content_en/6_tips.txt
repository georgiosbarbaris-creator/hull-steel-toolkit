@@ tips|t_top|💡|30 golden tips|From real experience
1. The best kit is the one you have WITH you when you need it. A small kit in your bag, a medium one in the car, a big one at home.
2. Don't save it "for when it happens". Use your gear once on a camping trip so you know it works.
3. Keep the fuel tank always above half.
4. Charge your phone every night, keep the powerbank always full.
5. Keep cash at home. Without power, cards and ATMs don't work.
6. Write the important phone numbers on paper.
7. Keep shoes and a torch next to the bed (earthquake at night = broken glass).
8. Keys in a fixed spot next to the door.
9. Learn where to shut off gas, water, power – and show everyone.
10. Take a first aid course. It is the most useful skill of your life.
11. Fix furniture to the wall.
12. Smoke and CO detector. Test every month.
13. Fill bottles with water in the empty spaces of the freezer.
14. Every time you go to the supermarket, buy 1–2 extra things for your stock. In a few months you have 2 weeks.
15. A whistle on every backpack and keyring. It costs very little, it saves lives.
16. Download offline maps of your area to your phone now.
17. Turn on Medical ID (iPhone: Health → Medical ID / Android: Emergency information) so it shows on the lock screen.
18. Pick a relative in another town as your "contact centre".
19. SMS gets through when calls don't.
20. Take photos of your home and belongings (for insurance/compensation).
21. If you leave home, leave a note: where you went, when.
22. Learn your nearest open assembly area and shelter.
23. Follow official instructions (112, Civil Protection), not rumours on social media.
24. In a crisis: first YOU are safe, then you help others (like the mask on the plane).
25. Panic is the biggest enemy. STOP – breathe – think.
26. Never touch unknown ammunition, fallen cables, or flood water with electricity in it.
27. No charcoal brazier, camping gas stove or generator inside the house. Ever.
28. Don't eat wild mushrooms.
29. Get to know your neighbours. In a disaster, the neighbour arrives first.
30. Practise with your family: "Earthquake now! Where do we go?". Twice a year.

@@ tips|t_more|🧠|Powerful survival tricks|Small secrets that make a big difference
## Before it hits (when a warning comes)
1. Fill the bathtub and containers with water.
2. Charge EVERYTHING: phones, powerbank, power station, flashlights, laptop.
3. Fill the fuel tank and withdraw cash (small notes).
4. Put bottles of water in the freezer: they keep the fridge cold and then become drinking water.
5. Set the fridge and freezer to the coldest setting.
## Fridge without power
- DO NOT open it. A closed fridge stays cold ~4 hours. A full freezer ~48 hours, half full ~24.
- Order of eating: fridge first, then freezer, canned and dry food LAST.
! Meat, fish, milk, eggs more than 2 hours in the heat: throw them away. If in doubt, throw it out. Diarrhoea in a crisis is dangerous.
## Cooking with half the fuel
- Retained-heat cooking: boil rice, pasta or pulses 5–10 minutes, put the lid on and wrap the pot in a sleeping bag or blankets. It cooks by itself in 1–2 hours. You save up to 2/3 of the gas.
- Soak pulses overnight: half the boiling time.
- Pressure cooker = less fuel and less water.
- Always use a lid. Without a lid you use twice the gas.
- Warm a can in the sun or on the bonnet of a running car (opened slightly, not sealed).
## Phone battery
- In the family: ONE phone on at a time, the others off. Swap every few hours.
- In the cold the battery “drops”: keep the phone inside your jacket, close to your body. When sleeping, inside the sleeping bag.
- An old phone with offline maps, this app and a charger = a spare in a bag in the backpack.
## The car is a tool
- Shelter, heating, charger, radio, lights for signalling.
- Heating: engine 10 minutes every hour, with the window slightly open.
! Snow or mud in the exhaust = carbon monoxide inside the car. Clear it before starting the engine.
## House without heating
- Insulation from household items: cardboard on the floor, blankets on the windows, foam or bubble wrap on the glass (spray with water and it sticks).
- Pitch a tent inside the room: a small space = it warms up from breath and bodies.
- Flowerpot-and-candle “heater”: gives very little heat and is a fire risk. Better: body, sleeping bag, one room.
## Water and hygiene
- Hand sanitiser BEFORE eating and AFTER the toilet. In disasters diarrhoea kills more people than we think.
- Baby wipes = a bath without water.
- A hanging bottle with a hole in the cap = a tap for hand washing with very little water.
- Washing water → toilet. Nothing is thrown away.
## Communication and searching
- If you leave home: write with a marker on paper on the door “We are 3, all OK, gone to …, date”.
- SMS instead of a call. Keep it short: “OK. Home. Tomorrow 10:00 at the meeting point.”
- 3 whistle blasts / 3 flashes / 3 fires = HELP all over the world.
## Health
- Always keep 2 weeks’ extra supply of the medicines you take daily (talk to your doctor).
- Spare prescription glasses in the backpack. Without glasses many people cannot do anything.
- Keep on paper: blood group, allergies, medicines, phone numbers.
## Mind
- A daily schedule: times for food, water, sleep, chores. Routine keeps you calm.
- Give children jobs (“you are in charge of the flashlights”). They feel useful and are less afraid.
- Help your neighbour. In crises neighbourhoods survive, not loners.

@@ tips|t_kids|🧒|For kids|Simple words for little heroes
- Earthquake: "I drop, I get under the table, I hold on". Then I go out only with a grown-up.
- Fire: "I go outside, I don't hide, I don't go back in". Never under the bed or in a wardrobe.
- Smoke: "I go low, on hands and knees".
- My clothes caught fire: "Stop – drop – roll".
- I am lost: "I stay where I am, I hug a tree, I whistle 3 times". I am not afraid of the people looking for me, even if they shout my name.
- I found something strange made of metal: "I DON'T touch it, I walk away, I tell a grown-up".
- Phone: "I call 112 and say my name, where I am, what happened".
- I know Mum's or Dad's phone number by heart.
- I know my family's meeting place.
+ Make it a game: "Who finds the safe spot in the room first?".
