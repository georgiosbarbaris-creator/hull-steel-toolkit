2. COVER your head and neck. Get under a strong table or desk.

## If you are in bed
- Stay there. Turn face down and cover your head with the pillow.
## If you are outside
- Go to an open space, away from buildings, poles, power lines and trees.

@@ now|trapped|🧱|Trapped under rubble|How they find you and how you hold on
+ Rescuers search for DAYS. Many people come out alive after 3, 5 or even more days. Your job is to hold on and to be heard.
## Right away
1. DO NOT light a fire, match or lighter – gas and dust can catch fire.
2. Cover your nose and mouth with cloth or a T-shirt (dust).
3. Move as little as possible, so you do not raise dust or bring down more debris. Do not pull on beams that hold things up.
4. Check yourself for bleeding – press hard with cloth.
## Make them hear you
1. TAP on a pipe, column or wall: 3 taps, pause, 3 again. Sound travels far through concrete and metal.
2. Whistle: 3 blasts, pause, again.
3. SHOUT only when you hear rescuers nearby. Shouting tires you and makes you breathe in dust.
4. When you hear rescuers call for “silence” (they stop the machines to listen): then tap or shout.
## Your mobile phone
- If it has signal: 112 – say exactly where you are (floor, room, who else is with you). If the call does not go through, send an SMS to 112 or to your family.
- Then low-power mode, low brightness. Turn the signal on for a short time every 30–60 minutes. Use its flashlight only briefly.
## Hold on
- Whatever water you have, drink it little by little but regularly.
- Keep your body warm: any cloth you find, underneath you and on top.
- Keep your mind busy: count, pray, think of your loved ones, talk to others who are trapped. Tell yourself: “They are looking for me. They will find me.”
- Sleep when you can – but tap again when you wake up.

- A strong or long earthquake while you are near the sea.
- The sea suddenly pulls back and the seabed shows.
- A loud roar from the sea.

- Inside the harbour: leave the boat and go to high ground on land.

2. Open or break the SIDE window (not the windscreen).

- Feet first, pointing downstream, on your back. Grab something fixed. Do not fight against the current.

@@ now|flood_road|🌊|Flooded road – car in the water|Turn back – do not drown
! Most flood deaths happen INSIDE CARS that tried to drive through water. NEVER cross a road, stream or underpass with flowing water.
## How much water is dangerous
- 15 cm of flowing water: knocks a person down.
- 30 cm: lifts and sweeps away most cars.
- 60 cm: sweeps away even 4x4s and vans.
- You do not know if the road is still there underneath – water eats away the asphalt. You cannot see open manholes.
- Underpasses: they fill up within a few minutes. They are traps.
## What to do
1. You see water on the road: TURN BACK. Look for a higher road, or stop on high ground and wait.
2. Do not park next to streams, dry riverbeds or under bridges.
3. If the car stalls in rising water: get out IMMEDIATELY and go to high ground. Leave the car.
## If the car falls or is swept into the water: B-W-O-C
1. BELT: undo your seat belt at once.
2. WINDOW: open the side window IMMEDIATELY, before the electrics fail. If it does not open, break the side window with a safety hammer or belt cutter (in the corner of the glass). NOT the windscreen – it does not break.
3. OUT: through the window, at once. DO NOT wait for it to fill up, do not try to open a door, do not phone first.
4. CHILDREN: the older ones out first (so they can help), then the small ones with you.
+ Buy a safety hammer with a belt cutter TODAY and keep it where the driver can reach it (not in the glovebox or the back). It costs as much as a coffee.
## If you fall into the water
- Lie on your back with your feet first, pointing downstream – so you push obstacles away with your feet.
- Do not fight the current. Swim at an angle towards the bank.
- Hold on to something fixed and shout / whistle for help.

- Insulin and some medicines spoil in the heat. Special cooling cases that you soak in water keep insulin cool for days without a fridge.

4. Give water to drink ONLY if they are fully awake and able to swallow.

2. Run the engine 10 minutes every hour for heat, with a window slightly open.

@@ now|micro|🏠|Home without heating – microclimate|Your body is the heater
+ When the power is off for many hours in winter, the house becomes a big fridge. You do not heat the whole house. You heat a SMALL space around your body.
## What is a microclimate (in simple words)
- Your body gives off heat all the time, like a small heater (about as much as a 100 W bulb).
- In a big room this heat is lost. In a small closed space (sleeping bag, tent) it stays around you and warms the air.
- The smaller and better insulated the space, the warmer it is. That is why we want a good sleeping bag: so our heat does not “escape”.
- In northern countries (Scandinavia, Canada) people do this when the power goes off: they set up a tent INSIDE the house.
## How to set it up – step by step
1. Choose the smallest room, with few windows, if possible an inner room or one facing the sun.
2. Close the other rooms. Blankets on the windows, towels under the doors, curtains closed.
3. SET UP A TENT inside the room (no pegs needed – put weights in the corners). If you have no tent: a “cave” made of a table covered with blankets, or blankets hung around the bed.
4. INSULATION FROM BELOW: never directly on tiles or marble. Rug → mat (foam) → air mattress or mattress → blanket. See “Insulation from below”.
5. Inside the tent: a sleeping bag for each person. Sleep close to each other – every person is one more heater.
6. Foil blanket (space blanket) over the tent or under the mat, shiny side in: it sends the heat back.
7. In the daytime: stay in the same room, dressed in layers, with a hat. Get into the sleeping bags even while sitting.
## Head OUT of the sleeping bag
! Do not put your nose and mouth inside the sleeping bag. Your breath gives off a lot of moisture that stays in the filling – the sleeping bag gets wet inside and freezes.
- Keep your face out. Wear a hat, pull the hood tight around your face so only a small hole is left for nose and mouth.
- A buff or scarf on your neck if your face is very cold.
## Insulation from below
- The ground/floor “sucks” heat away 10 times faster than air. Insulation below is more important than a blanket on top.
- Best: foam mat + air mattress with R-value 4–5+ on top. R-values add up.
- Improvised: cardboard (many layers), rugs, a bed mattress on the floor, sofa cushions, thick folded blankets, polystyrene.
- Foil blanket under the mat: sends heat back and blocks the damp from the floor.
## Tricks for a warm sleep
- Eat something fatty/hot before sleep (nuts, chocolate, soup). The body needs “fuel”.
- Do 2 minutes of exercise (squats) before getting into the sleeping bag – you go in warm, not cold.
- A bottle of hot (not boiling) water, tightly closed, between your thighs or at your feet.
- Dry wool socks ONLY for sleeping. Not clothes you sweated in.
- Put the next day's clothes inside the sleeping bag – in the morning they are warm.
- Pee before you sleep. Keep a container nearby for the night.
! NEVER use a charcoal brazier, barbecue, gas stove or generator inside the house or inside the tent. Carbon monoxide (CO) – no smell, kills in your sleep. The tent inside the house is only for body heat.
+ Put a battery CO alarm in the room where you sleep.

@@ now|bodystove|🔥|Your body is the stove|How to keep warm without heating
+ The most important secret about cold: a sleeping bag and a blanket DO NOT make heat. They just KEEP the heat your body makes. You are the stove.
## How it works (simply)
- Lying down, your body gives off heat like an 80–100 W light bulb. All night, without electricity.
- Food is the fuel. Without food the stove goes out.
- Water keeps the stove running. When you are dehydrated you get cold faster.
- Your job: DO NOT lose the heat – from below (the ground), to the air, through wet clothes.
## Add “fuel” before sleep
1. Eat something fatty and sweet before lying down: nuts, halva, chocolate, peanut butter, cheese. Fats “burn” slowly, all night.
2. Drink something hot and sweet (tea with honey, cocoa).
3. Do 20–30 squats or push-ups just before getting into the sleeping bag – to warm up, NOT to sweat.
4. Go to the toilet before sleep. The body uses heat to keep a full bladder warm.
## The hot water bottle trick
1. Boil water and fill a metal bottle or a bottle that can take boiling water (e.g. Nalgene). Screw it VERY tight and turn it upside down to check it does not drip.
2. Wrap it in a sock or towel – NEVER bare against the skin.
3. Put it in the sleeping bag 10 minutes before you get in, to warm it up.
4. Then place it between your thighs (large veins pass there and all your blood warms up) or at your feet.
- One litre of boiling water stays warm for 6–8 hours inside the sleeping bag. In the morning you drink it – warm water without lighting a fire.
- A classic rubber hot water bottle does the same job: hot (not boiling) water, squeeze out the air, close it well.
- A thermos flask of boiling water next to the bed: a hot drink during the night and in the morning.
! Babies, small children, elderly people, diabetics: they do not feel burns well. Only a lukewarm bottle, well wrapped, and keep checking.
## Do not lose heat
- You lose the most FROM BELOW: the floor and the ground “suck out” heat. Sleeping mat, cardboard, blankets UNDER you – more than you put on top.
- Wear a hat in bed too. It covers your head and ears, which lose a lot.
- Dry socks for sleeping, not tight (tightness cuts off blood flow and you get cold).
- Do not breathe inside the sleeping bag: the moisture of your breath makes it damp and it loses its warmth.
- Wet or sweaty clothes = a fridge. Change at once.
- Sleep together, in ONE room. Children in the middle. Each person is a 100 W stove – 4 people = 400 W in a small room.
- Small room, doors closed, blankets or cardboard on the windows, a towel along the gap under the door.
! NO alcohol to warm up. It gives a false feeling of warmth but widens the blood vessels and you lose heat faster.
! NO camping gas heater, brazier, charcoal or barbecue indoors – carbon monoxide kills in your sleep and has no smell.
## Hypothermia: when someone is in danger
- Early stage: shivering, cold hands, red nose. Put on dry clothes, give food, a hot sweet drink, movement.
- DANGER: stumbling, slurred speech, cannot do up buttons, irritable or apathetic.
! If they STOP shivering and are confused or drowsy: SEVERE HYPOTHERMIA. Call 112/166. Wrap them up (underneath too), keep them lying flat, handle gently, warm the trunk (armpits, chest) – NO rubbing of arms and legs.

@@ now|homewater|🛁|Water at home: bathtub and hidden stores|Fill up BEFORE the water is cut
+ As soon as you hear of an earthquake, war, storm or water cut: FILL THE BATHTUB IMMEDIATELY. The water in the pipes can be cut within a few hours.
## How much water a bathtub holds
- Small bathtub (150 × 70 cm): about 120–150 litres.
- Standard bathtub (170 × 70 cm): about 150–200 litres.
- Large or corner bathtub: 250–350 litres.
- To work out yours: length × width × water depth in decimetres (10 cm = 1 dm) × 0.7 (because the sides slope). Example: 16 × 6 × 3.5 × 0.7 ≈ 235 litres.
## How many days it lasts
- For DRINKING only (4 litres/person/day): 200 litres = 4 people for 12 days.
- For EVERYTHING (drinking, cooking, washing, toilet ~10–15 litres/person/day): 200 litres = 4 people for 3–5 days. So save water from the first hour.
## The right steps
1. Quickly wash the bathtub (without many chemicals) and rinse.
2. Put in the plug and seal over it with cling film or tape – plugs slowly leak. Test this once NOW.
3. Fill to just below the overflow.
4. Cover with plastic sheeting or a board against dust, animals and CHILDREN.
5. Take water with a clean jug – not with your hands in it.
! A full bathtub = drowning risk for a small child. Keep the bathroom door closed, the tub covered, and supervise.
! Bathtub water is NOT safe to drink. Boil for 1 minute or disinfect (bleach: 2 drops/litre, wait 30 minutes) or filter. Untreated: only for the toilet and washing.
+ There are special bathtub water bags (100–400 L) with a tap: they keep the water clean for drinking. Cheap and useful.
## ALSO fill these
[ ] Pots, bottles, jerrycans, buckets – anything with a lid
[ ] Sinks with the plug in (for washing)
[ ] Washing machine: run a wash NOW, later you will not be able to
## Hidden water stores at home
- Electric water heater: 60–120 litres. Switch OFF its power first. Close the supply, open a hot tap to let air in, and drain water from the valve at the bottom. Boil before drinking.
- Solar water heater on the roof: 120–200 litres. A big store we forget about.
- Pipes: close the main valve, open the highest tap to let air in, collect from the lowest: 5–20 litres.
- Toilet cistern (NOT the bowl): 6–9 litres of clean water – if it has no colour tablets/chemicals.
- Ice from the freezer and liquid from canned vegetables/fruit.
- Rainwater from gutters: for washing. For drinking only with a filter + boiling.
! If the authorities say the mains water is contaminated, DO NOT fill up from the tap – and close the main valve so dirty water does not get into the water heater.
## Toilet with little water
- A 4–6 litre bucket poured quickly and sharply into the bowl = a flush. Only for “number two”.
- For “number one”: a rubbish bag inside the bowl + cat litter, sawdust or ash. Tie it up and change it.

@@ now|war|🛡️|War – air raid|Protecting civilians
## Siren / air raid warning
1. Go at once to a shelter, basement or underground station.
2. If there is no time: an inner room or corridor, low floor, away from windows. The “2 walls” rule: put two walls between you and the street.
3. A bathroom or corridor with no windows is often the best place in a flat.
4. If you are outside: lie face down in a ditch or behind a low wall, hands on your head, mouth slightly open.
5. Stay in the shelter until the all-clear signal.
! DO NOT go out to look or take photos. DO NOT post videos showing positions or military vehicles on social media – it can help the enemy and put you in danger.
## Preparing the home
- Tape in an X shape on the glass or anti-shatter film, curtains closed.
- Blackout at night if the authorities ask (blankets/black plastic).
- Supplies for 2 weeks: water, food, medicines, batteries, cash.
- A backpack ready by the door for each member.
- Copies of documents (paper + USB), photos of the family members.
## If the family gets separated
- Agree in advance on 2 meeting points (one nearby, one outside the city) and a relative far away who will be the “contact centre”.
- For children: a card in the pocket with name, phone numbers, blood group, allergies.
## Moving – checkpoints
- Stay calm, hands visible, answer briefly and truthfully. Documents in an easy pocket.
- Move by day, on known roads, follow the official evacuation routes.
- Do not wear camouflage or clothes that look military.
## Suspicious objects / ammunition
! DO NOT touch, pick up or kick an unknown metal object, shell, mine or fallen drone.
1. Stop. Go back the way you came, in your own footsteps.
2. Mark the spot from a distance (stones, cloth).
3. Report to 100 / 112.
4. Teach children: “I don't touch, I go away, I tell a grown-up”.
## Drones
- If you hear a drone close by: go into a building or under trees/cover, do not look up and do not run into open ground.
+ Find out now where the nearest shelters and basements are in your neighbourhood, at work and at your children's school.

@@ now|nuclear|☢️|Nuclear explosion – radiation|Get inside – Stay inside – Stay tuned
## If you see a flash / explosion
1. DO NOT look at the flash.
2. Drop face down behind anything that protects you, cover your head and put your hands under your body.
3. Stay down until the blast wave passes (it may come seconds or even minutes later).
## After: the radioactive dust (fallout)
- The dangerous dust falls within 10–30 minutes. You have a little time to get into a good building.
1. GET INSIDE the strongest building you can find (concrete, brick).
2. Go to the basement or the MIDDLE of the building, away from outer walls and the roof. In a block of flats: the middle floors in the centre are good.
3. Close doors, windows, air conditioning, ventilation.
4. STAY INSIDE at least 24 hours, unless the authorities say otherwise. Radiation drops fast: after 7 hours ~10 times less, after 2 days ~100 times less.
5. STAY TUNED to a battery radio and 112.
! Do not get into a car to leave unless you are told to. A car does not protect from radiation.
## Decontamination (if you were outside)
1. Before you enter the clean space, take off your outer clothes – this removes most of the dust.
2. Put them in a plastic bag, tied, away from people.
3. Shower with soap and shampoo. NO conditioner (it sticks the dust to the hair). Do not scrub hard.
4. If there is no shower: wipe face, hands, hair with a wet cloth. Blow your nose, clean ears and eyelashes.
5. Clean clothes from a closed wardrobe.
## Food and water
- Food in sealed packs and water in sealed bottles are safe. Wipe the outside before you open them.
- Do not eat fruit/vegetables from the garden or milk from local animals without instructions from the authorities.
## Potassium iodide (KI)
! ONLY when the authorities say so. It protects ONLY the thyroid from radioactive iodine. It does not protect from anything else. Do not take it “just in case”. Never take iodine antiseptic (Betadine) by mouth.
## Accident at a nuclear power plant (e.g. in a nearby country)
- Usually there is time. Follow the instructions: stay inside, windows closed, stay informed. Do not panic.
+ A battery or wind-up radio is the most important tool here. Also keep water/food for 3–14 days inside.

## What to do
1. Get inside a building at once. Close doors, windows, fireplace.

@@ now|sea|⚓|Shipwreck – man overboard|Lifejacket, warmth, being found
## Before you abandon ship
1. Put on a lifejacket and warm clothes – as many as possible, they keep heat even when wet.
2. Take the grab bag, EPIRB, SART, portable VHF, water, medicines.
3. Send MAYDAY (VHF Ch16 / DSC) with your position.
4. Get into the liferaft directly if you can, without getting wet. If you must jump: from low down, arms crossed over the lifejacket, hold your nose, feet together, look ahead.
## In the water
- First minute: cold water shock – do not panic, control your breathing (1-10-1 rule: 1 minute to breathe, 10 minutes of useful movement, ~1 hour before hypothermia in cold water).
- Do not swim without need. H.E.L.P. position: knees to chest, arms tight around the body.
- With others: a tight circle (huddle), children/injured in the middle.
- Climb onto anything that floats – less body in the water = less heat loss.
## In the liferaft
1. Cut the painter, get clear of the ship, stream the sea anchor (drogue).
2. Pick up other survivors, close the entrance, dry the floor.
3. Take seasickness medicine AT ONCE (vomiting = losing water).
4. Activate EPIRB/SART. Set lookout watches.
5. NO water in the first 24 hours (except the injured), then ~0.5 litre/person/day. Collect rainwater.
! NEVER seawater, NEVER urine. Without water, do not eat.
- Flares ONLY when you see/hear a ship or aircraft. Signal mirror by day.
## Man overboard (MOB) from a boat
1. Shout “Man overboard – starboard/port!”.
2. Throw a lifebuoy/light buoy at once.
3. ONE person only keeps watching and pointing with the arm all the time.
4. Press MOB on the GPS. Call VHF/112/108 Coast Guard.
+ Coast Guard phone: 108. At sea, 112 also works where there is signal.

## The STOP rule
1. STOP. Sit down. Drink water. Panic kills more than the cold.
2. THINK: where did I come from? How many hours of light do I have? What do I have with me?
3. OBSERVE: roads, sounds of cars, streams, peaks, power poles.
4. PLAN: Usually the best thing is to STAY WHERE YOU ARE and let them find you.
## What to do
- Call 112 (it works on any network). Send your coordinates from the app's Compass.
- Save battery: turn off mobile data, screen brightness low, turn on the phone once an hour.
- Make yourself visible: bright clothes in an open spot, whistle 3 times, smoke by day, light at night.
- Before dark: build a shelter from the wind, with insulation from the ground (branches, leaves). See “Shelter”.
- If you decide to move: follow a stream or path DOWNHILL – but watch out for cliffs and waterfalls. Leave marks behind you.
! Do not walk at night. Do not enter a gorge if you do not know where it comes out.
+ Always tell someone where you are going and when you will be back.

@@ now|cyber|💻|Cyberattack|No banks, cards or internet – and lots of scams
## What it is (in plain words)
- Someone "breaks into" the computers that run electricity, water, banks, phones or hospitals – or gets into your own phone and accounts.
- The European Union asks every household to manage 72 hours on its own. A cyberattack is one of the main reasons.
- Two kinds: (a) a BIG attack on infrastructure = like a power or bank outage for everyone. (b) An attack on YOU = stolen money, passwords, phone.
## Signs that a big attack is happening
- ATMs and cards don't work, banking apps are "down".
- Petrol stations, supermarkets and pharmacies take cash only.
- Internet, mobile network or power drop in many areas at once, with no bad weather.
- A flood of scary messages, videos and "leaks" on social media. Many are fake – on purpose.
## Big attack: what I do NOW
1. Stay calm. Treat it like a power cut – see also the cards "Power cut" and "No internet and no phone".
2. Get news ONLY from official sources: battery radio (ERT), 112 messages, civilprotection.gov.gr. Not from forwarded messages.
3. DON'T forward anything you haven't checked. Disinformation is part of the attack – it wants panic.
4. Use the cash from your bag only for essentials. Don't empty the shelves.
5. Phone charged and on power saving. Calls only for emergencies – 112 first.
6. At ATMs and in queues, watch out for "helpful strangers" who want to see your card or PIN.
7. Never enter passwords on pages someone sent you "to check if you were affected".
! In every crisis scams multiply: fake SMS "from gov.gr, 112, the power company, your bank, a courier", fake benefits, fake charities.
## I've been hacked – what to do, in order
1. Your bank AT ONCE: call the number on the back of your card and ask to block the card and transactions. Every minute counts.
2. Disconnect the device from the internet (airplane mode, Wi-Fi off). Don't switch it off – it holds evidence.
3. From ANOTHER, clean device change passwords: email first, then the bank, then the rest. A different password for each.
4. Turn on 2FA (two-step verification) and tap "sign out of all devices".
5. If your phone suddenly says "No service" while the area has signal: call your mobile provider at once. It may be a SIM swap – they steal your number to receive your bank codes.
6. Screenshots of messages, amounts, times, numbers. Don't delete anything.
7. Report it to the Cyber Crime Division: 11188 (24 hours, free) or ccu@cybercrimeunit.gov.gr.
8. Warn friends and family: "If you get a money request from my phone or Viber, it's not me."
! If they demand a ransom (ransomware: "we locked your files – pay"), DON'T pay. You get no guarantee. Report it and restore from your backup.
## The 5 golden rules against scams
- STOP – THINK – CHECK. Hurry is their weapon. Anything that "expires in 1 hour" is suspicious.
- Banks, police and the state NEVER ask for passwords, PINs or the one-time code (OTP) you got by SMS. Never.
- Public bodies don't send SMS with a link to pay a fine or give card details. Go to the official site or app yourself.
- Hang up and call back YOURSELF, on the number you know (back of the card, official site) – not the one they gave you.
- NEVER install an app because someone on the phone told you to (AnyDesk, TeamViewer, a "security app"). You'd be handing them your phone.
! A voice that sounds like your child or parent ("I lost my phone, send money") may be made with AI. Hang up, call the known number or ask for the family's secret word.
