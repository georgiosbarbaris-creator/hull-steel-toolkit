@@ wild|w_rule3|3️⃣|The rule of 3|What is most urgent
- 3 MINUTES without air (or with heavy bleeding).
- 3 HOURS without protection in harsh weather (cold, rain, heatwave).
- 3 DAYS without water.
- 3 WEEKS without food.
## So the order is:
1. Safety and first aid.
2. Shelter – warmth/cooling.
3. Water.
4. Signals so they can find you.
5. Food (last!).
+ And above all: STAY CALM and keep the will to live. Survivors are usually not the strongest, but those who do not give up.

@@ wild|w_mind|🧠|Mind – staying calm|The most important tool
- Box breathing 4-4-4-4: breathe in 4, hold 4, breathe out 4, hold 4. Three times. It calms panic.
- Take SMALL steps: “now I build the shelter”, not “how will I get out of this”.
- Routine: set times for water, food, sleep, checks. Routine keeps the mind steady.
- Give everyone a job, children too. Work drives fear away.
- Talk, keep a diary, pray if it helps you. Think of the people waiting for you.
- After the crisis: anxiety, nightmares and anger are normal. Talk to someone. Ask for help if it lasts.

@@ wild|w_shelter|🏕️|Shelter in the wild|From wind, rain and the ground
## Where
- Sheltered from wind (behind a rock, inside a forest), NOT in a stream or dry riverbed, NOT under dead branches, NOT on a summit.
- Near water but not next to it (50+ m).
## How
1. Insulate from the ground FIRST: 20–30 cm of branches, leaves, pine needles.
2. Small shelter – you heat a small space with your body.
3. With a tarp/plastic sheet: rope between 2 trees, sheet over it like an “A”, windward side closed.
4. With nothing: a ridge pole leaning on a trunk, branches along the sides like ribs, 50+ cm of leaves on top.
5. Entrance facing away from the wind.
## In snow
- Dig a hollow in the snow or under a fir tree. Make an air hole. Snow insulates.
+ A large bin bag with a hole for the head = waterproof and windproof in 10 seconds.

@@ wild|w_water|💧|Water: where to find it|Treat all water from nature as contaminated
## How much I need
- 2–3 litres a day for drinking, per person. In heat or hard work: 4–6 litres.
- Plus 1–2 litres for cooking and hand washing.
+ Do not “save” water in the bottle while you are thirsty. Drink little and often, but DRINK. Cut effort and sweat, not water.
! Signs of dehydration: dark urine, headache, dizziness, dry mouth. Children and elderly people dehydrate faster.
## At home (when the water is cut off)
- Water heater: switch off the power, close the supply, open the drain tap at the bottom. 40–120 litres of clean water.
- Toilet cistern (the tank, NOT the bowl), if it has no chemical tablets.
- Pipes: open the tap at the highest point of the house and collect from the lowest.
- Canned fruit and vegetables (their liquid), ice cubes in the freezer.
- As soon as you hear the water will be cut: fill the bathtub, pots and bottles.
! Water from radiators, pools, aquariums: only for flushing and washing, NOT for drinking.
## In the wild
- Running water (spring, stream) is better than still water. A spring where it comes out of the rock is best.
- Look low: gorges, valley floors, green vegetation, plane trees, reeds, brambles.
- Animal trails going downhill, birds flying low and straight at dusk, bees and wasps (water within 1–2 km).
- Dry stream: dig on the outside of a bend, at the lowest point. If you find wet sand, dig 30–50 cm and wait for water to collect.
- On the beach: dig behind the first sand dune, above the high-tide line. The first water to collect is less salty.
## Collect from the sky
1. Rain: spread plastic, canvas or a tarp sloping into a container. Let the first minutes rinse off the dust.
2. Dew: at dawn wipe the grass with a cotton cloth or T-shirt and wring it out. Half a litre an hour with good dew.
3. Snow and ice: melt them in a pot. Put a little water in the bottom first so it does not burn. Ice gives more water than snow.
! Do not eat snow – it lowers your body temperature and causes hypothermia.
## Transpiration bag
1. Put a clear plastic bag around a branch with lots of green leaves, in the sun.
2. Tie the opening tightly. Put a small pebble inside so the water collects in one corner.
3. In 4–6 hours you have a little clean water. Use many bags on different trees.
! Not on poisonous plants (e.g. oleander). If you do not know the plant, do not drink it.
## Solar still
1. Dig a pit 60 cm wide and 50 cm deep, in the sun, in damp ground.
2. Put a container in the centre. Around it put green leaves or salty/dirty water or urine.
3. Cover with clear plastic, seal the edges with soil, put a stone in the centre, right above the container.
4. Water evaporates, forms drops on the plastic and drips CLEAN into the container.
+ It gives little (0.2–0.5 litres a day). It is for emergencies, not to live on.
## Water you DO NOT drink
! NEVER seawater, urine or alcohol – they dehydrate you faster.
! Flood water: it contains sewage, oil and chemicals. Not even treated, if there is another option.
! Avoid water with dead animals nearby, with green-blue scum (cyanobacteria – the toxins are NOT removed by boiling), downstream of villages, sprayed fields, mines, factories.

@@ wild|w_water2|🚰|Water filtering & treatment|Boiling, bleach, tablets, filters, DIY filter
## The 3 steps
1. CLARIFY: remove mud and debris (settling, cloth, sand filter).
2. FILTER: remove bacteria and parasites (0.1–0.2 μm filter).
3. DISINFECT: kill viruses too (boiling, bleach, tablets, UV).
+ Cloudy water? Always do step 1 first. Mud “hides” germs from bleach and sunlight, and clogs filters.
## 1. Boiling – the surest method
1. Pass the water through a cloth if it is cloudy.
2. Bring to a ROLLING boil (big bubbles) for 1 minute. Above 2000 m altitude: 3 minutes.
3. Let it cool covered. To remove the “flat” taste, pour it back and forth between containers.
- Kills ALL germs: bacteria, viruses, parasites.
! Does NOT remove salt, chemicals, oil, heavy metals or algae toxins.
+ No pot: a plastic bottle filled COMPLETELY with water, hung over embers (not flames), boils without melting. Or heat stones in the fire and drop them into a container of water (not river stones – they explode).
## 2. Household bleach
1. ONLY plain bleach, unscented, no detergent (5–6% sodium hypochlorite, it says on the label).
2. Clear water: 2 drops per litre (8 drops per 4 litres).
3. Cloudy or very cold water: 4 drops per litre.
4. Stir and wait 30 minutes (cold water: 1 hour).
5. It should smell SLIGHTLY of chlorine. If it does not, add the same dose again and wait another 15 minutes.
- Kills bacteria and viruses. Some parasites (Cryptosporidium) survive.
+ Keep a small dropper bottle of bleach in your backpack. Bleach loses strength after 6–12 months – replace it.
## 3. Tablets
- Chlorine dioxide (e.g. Micropur MP1, Aquamira): kills parasites TOO. Wait 30 minutes for bacteria/viruses and 4 hours for Cryptosporidium. The best tablet for the backpack.
- NaDCC / chlorine (e.g. Aquatabs): fast, cheap, like bleach.
- Iodine: 5 drops of 2% iodine tincture per litre, 30 minutes. NOT for pregnant women, children, thyroid conditions or use for more than a few weeks.
+ ALWAYS follow the package instructions. Check the expiry date.
## 4. Portable filters
- 0.1 μm hollow-fibre filters (Sawyer Squeeze/Mini, LifeStraw, Katadyn BeFree): stop bacteria and parasites, not viruses. Light, cheap, filter thousands of litres.
- Ceramic filters (e.g. Katadyn): tough, cleaned with a brush.
- Activated carbon filters: improve taste and remove some chemicals – on their own they do not kill germs.
1. Filter from the cleanest spot, not from the bottom.
2. Do not let the “dirty” end touch the clean bottle.
3. Backflush when the flow drops.
! FREEZING DAMAGES fibre filters without showing it. In winter sleep with the filter inside your sleeping bag.
! In floods or near villages (sewage = viruses): after filtering ALSO add bleach or boil.
- UV pen (e.g. SteriPEN): kills all germs in 90″ in CLEAR water. Needs a battery.
## 5. Sun – SODIS
1. Clear PET plastic bottle up to 2 litres, no label, NO scratches.
2. Fill it with CLEAR water (if you can read letters through it, it is fine).
3. Lay it in the sun, ideally on metal sheeting or a roof, for 6 hours. Cloudy: 2 days. Rain: it does not work.
+ Last resort, but better than nothing. Needs no fuel.
## DIY bottle filter
1. Cut the bottom off a 1.5–2 litre plastic bottle and turn it upside down (cap at the bottom, pierced or unscrewed).
2. Put cotton wool, gauze or a piece of T-shirt in the neck.
3. On top, 3–5 cm of crushed CHARCOAL from your fire (not briquettes with chemicals).
4. Then fine sand, then coarse sand, fine gravel on the very top.
5. Pour water slowly. Throw away the water from the first 2–3 fillings (it washes out charcoal dust).
! This filter only CLARIFIES. It does NOT make water safe to drink. ALWAYS boil, add bleach or a tablet afterwards.
+ Quick fix: tie a sock or T-shirt into a funnel shape, fill it with sand and pour the water through.
## Salty or chemical water – distillation
- Only distillation removes salt and most chemicals.
1. Pot with seawater/dirty water on the fire.
2. Inside, in the centre, a mug standing on a stone (not touching the water).
3. Cover with the lid UPSIDE DOWN (handle pointing down, above the mug). Put cold water or snow on top of the lid.
4. The steam forms drops on the lid and drips clean into the mug.
+ Or: a tube/reed from the spout of a covered boiling container, leading to a container sitting in cold water.
## Quick table
- Cloudy water → settling + cloth → boiling or bleach.
- Mountain stream → 0.1 μm filter (or boiling).
- Flood water or water near a village → cloth → filter → AND bleach or boiling.
- Seawater → distillation only.
- Water with oil, chemicals, green scum → DO NOT drink it. Find another source.

@@ wild|w_fire|🔥|Fire: how to build it|Warmth, boiling, signal, morale
## The 3 materials – gather them BEFORE you light
- TINDER (catches from a spark): one big handful.
- KINDLING (match to pencil thickness): LOTS – two armfuls.
- FUEL WOOD (finger to arm thickness): enough for the whole night. Whatever you think, bring double.
+ The most common failure is too little kindling. That is where the fire dies.
## The best tinder
- Cotton balls with petroleum jelly: catch with one spark and burn 2–3 minutes. Put 10–20 balls in a small bag or tin – the No.1 item in your backpack.
- Fatwood (resin-soaked wood from a pine root or stump, smells of resin, reddish): lights even when wet.
- Pine resin running from the trunk.
- Bark of birch, cedar, cypress, grapevine – rub it into fluff.
- Dry grass, dry pine needles, bird nests, cattail (Typha) and thistle fluff.
- Shavings: carve a dry stick with the knife into thin “curls” that stay attached (feather stick).
- From home/car: paper, egg cartons, dryer lint, hand sanitiser gel (alcohol), crisps, candle wax, petroleum-jelly gauze from the pharmacy.
## Rain and damp
- Take dead branches that are STILL on the tree, not from the ground.
- Under fallen trunks, rocks and dense firs the wood is drier.
- Split thick wood: the inside is dry. Make feather sticks from it.
- Put damp wood AROUND the fire to dry before adding it.
- Make a base of sticks or bark, not on wet soil or snow.
- Dead branches that SNAP with a “crack” are dry. If they bend, they are damp.
## How to light it
1. Clear a 3 m circle of dry material. Away from trees, overhanging branches and tents. In strong wind – do not light.
2. Ring of stones (NOT river stones or wet stones – they explode) or dig a small pit.
3. Base of dry sticks. Tinder in the middle.
4. Around the tinder stand the kindling in a tent shape (tipi), leaving a “door” on the windward side.
5. Light the tinder from the windward side. Blow gently and steadily at the base, not from above.
6. When the kindling catches, slowly add bigger pieces. Do not smother it – fire needs air.
## Fire lays
- Tipi (tent): lights fast, strong flame. For warmth, boiling, signalling.
- Log cabin (crossed logs like a hut): stable, makes good embers. For cooking.
- Lean-to: one thick log toward the wind, kindling leaning on it. For windy conditions.
- Star: 5–6 thick logs like spokes, push them inward as they burn. Saves wood, lasts all night.
- Dakota (hidden): two 30 cm pits joined at the bottom by a tunnel. Fire in one, air from the other. Little smoke, little wood, not visible, wind-resistant.
## Warmth for sleeping
- Reflector wall behind the fire (logs or stones): turns the heat toward you.
- Sleep 1–1.5 m from a long, low fire, with the wall behind the fire or behind you.
- Hot stones (not from a river) wrapped in cloth in the sleeping bag, or a bottle of hot water.
! NEVER a fire, brazier, charcoal or gas stove inside a tent, car or closed room. Carbon monoxide: it has no smell and kills in your sleep.
## Safety and the law
! In Greece, during the fire season (1 May – 31 October) fires are banned in forests, fields and open country. Only in real danger to life, with extreme care.
! Always keep water or soil next to you. Never leave a burning fire.
! Putting it out: water, stir with a stick, water again, until it is COLD to the touch. Embers under the ash can reignite hours later.
+ If it gets away: do not try to put it out alone if it has grown. Move upwind and downhill, and call 199/112.

@@ wild|w_fire2|🪵|8 ways to start a fire|From the lighter to the bow drill
## 1. Lighter
- The easiest. Carry 2–3 simple flint-wheel lighters (e.g. BIC) – in different pockets.
- In the cold the gas will not flow: keep it in an inside pocket, close to your body.
- Got wet? Blow on it and rub the wheel on clothing until dry. Even empty, the wheel throws sparks onto petroleum-jelly cotton.
+ In wind: make a “cave” with your body and palms and light close to the tinder.
## 2. Matches
- Stormproof matches: burn in wind and relight after getting wet. In a waterproof case together with the striker.
- Ordinary matches: dip the heads in melted wax – they become waterproof. Scrape off the wax before lighting.
1. Hold the match with the head slightly down, so the flame climbs the stick.
2. Bring it straight to the tinder, sheltered from the wind.
## 3. Ferro rod
- Works wet, in wind, in cold, for thousands of strikes. The best backup – put one in your kit.
1. Put the tip of the rod INTO the tinder.
2. Hold the striker (or the knife spine) still against the rod.
3. PULL the rod back, do not push the striker forward – this way you do not scatter the tinder.
4. Sparks at 3,000 °C fall on the tinder. Blow gently.
+ With a magnesium rod: first scrape a coin-sized pile of shavings onto the tinder, then strike a spark.
## 4. Battery and steel wool
- You need very fine steel wool (grade 0000, not stainless).
1. Stretch out a piece of steel wool, fluff it up.
2. Touch the wool to both terminals of a 9V battery – it lights at once.
3. With an AA/AAA battery or powerbank: one thin strand of wool from one terminal to the other.
4. Drop the glowing wool into the tinder and blow.
! Never carry a 9V battery and steel wool in the same pocket – it can ignite by itself.
+ Car: the dashboard cigarette lighter lights paper, cotton or bark. Always away from petrol.
## 5. Lens and sun
- Magnifying glass, READING glasses (for close-up – glasses for short-sightedness do not work), binocular or camera lens, card-sized Fresnel lens.
- Also: a clear bottle or bag full of water, ice polished into a lens.
1. Only in strong sun, ideally 10:00–15:00.
2. Dark, dry tinder: char cloth, dry rotten wood (punk), blackened paper.
3. Hold the lens so the bright dot is as SMALL as possible, and STILL.
4. As soon as smoke and a red dot appear, put it into tinder and blow.
+ Never needs fuel. Keep a Fresnel lens in your wallet.
## 6. Flint, steel and char cloth
- High-carbon steel (old file, carbon knife spine) + hard stone with a sharp edge (flint, quartz).
1. Hold the stone with a piece of char cloth on it, just below the sharp edge.
2. Strike the steel downward, glancing off the edge of the stone. The sparks fall on the char cloth.
3. When the cloth shows a red dot, put it inside a “nest” of dry grass, close it and blow until it flames.
## Make char cloth (at home or on the fire)
1. Cut 100% cotton cloth (old jeans, T-shirt) into 5×5 cm squares.
2. Put them in a metal tin with one small hole in the lid.
3. Put the tin in the fire. Smoke comes out of the hole.
4. When the smoke stops, take it out and let it cool CLOSED.
5. The squares must be black, not ash. They catch with the smallest spark.
## 7. Bow drill – with nothing
- Hard. Needs practice at home, not your first try in an emergency. The wood must be BONE DRY.
- Good woods: willow, poplar, lime (linden), cedar, cypress, ivy root. Not resinous pine, not hard oak.
1. Spindle: straight stick 20 cm, thumb-thick, pointed at the top end, rounded at the bottom.
2. Hearth board: same wood, 2 cm thick. Make a small depression near the edge and a V-shaped notch (1/8 of the circle) from the edge to the centre of the depression.
3. Handhold on top: a stone with a hollow or hard wood. Lubricate with soap, fat or leaves.
4. Bow: a branch about arm length, slightly curved, with a slightly slack cord.
5. Wrap the cord once around the spindle. Put a leaf or bark under the notch.
6. Hold the board down with your foot, the hand with the handhold locked against your knee.
7. Saw with long, steady strokes, slowly at first, then fast and with pressure. When there is lots of smoke and black dust, do 10–20 more.
8. The dust in the notch becomes a red ember. Carry it ON THE LEAF to the grass nest and blow.
## 8. Fire from your fire – keep it alive
- The easiest lighting is the one you do not need: cover the embers with ash at night, uncover in the morning and add thin twigs.
- Carrying: an ember in a tin can with holes or in a dry tinder fungus, wrapped in green leaves.
## Always carry
[ ] 2 BIC lighters in different pockets
[ ] Ferro rod
[ ] Stormproof matches in a waterproof case
[ ] Petroleum-jelly cotton balls (10–20)
[ ] Card-sized Fresnel lens
[ ] A piece of fatwood or a candle
[ ] Knife for shavings (feather sticks)

@@ wild|w_car|🚙|Sleeping in the car – like an overlander|Warm, safe, no carbon monoxide
! NEVER sleep with the engine running. Carbon monoxide (CO) gets into the cabin, especially if snow blocks the exhaust. It has no smell and you do not wake up.
## Where I park
- Level ground (you sleep much better). Nose or rear into the wind, so it does not hit the side.
- NOT in a stream bed, under dead branches/trees, below a slope with loose rocks or avalanche risk, next to a cliff.
- Away from the road (lights, snowploughs), but somewhere you can get out if it snows. In snow: nose toward the exit.
- Tell someone where you are (send the coordinates from the “Compass”).
## Insulating the cabin
1. Insulate the WINDOWS: that is where most heat escapes. Cut panels of reflectix (foil bubble wrap) or foam underlay, shaped to each window. They press-fit, no glue.
2. Improvised: blankets, towels, foil blankets, cardboard held in by the doors/sun visors.
3. Curtain/blanket between the front seats and the rear – you heat only the sleeping area (microclimate).
4. On the floor/boot: foam pad + mattress. The car's metal is very cold.
## Ventilation – condensation
- Leave 1–2 windows open 1–2 cm (with a mesh or wind deflector). Without ventilation your breath turns to water and ice on the windows and sleeping bag.
- Microfibre towel for the windows in the morning. Wet clothes in a bag, not spread out in the cabin.
## Warmth
- The sleeping bag keeps you warm, not the engine. Winter in the north: comfort −15 to −20 °C, liner, foil blanket underneath.
- Hot-water bottle (heat water on a gas stove OUTSIDE the car).
- If you have a diesel heater: ONLY with proper installation, exhaust outside, and a CO detector inside. No CO detector, no heater.
- If you are stuck awake in snow: engine only 10 minutes every hour, exhaust clear of snow, window slightly open.
## Fuel and safety
! Petrol/diesel cans and gas canisters NEVER inside the cabin where you sleep. Fumes and fire. Outside, on the roof rack or in a closed external box.
! Never a gas stove, candle or brazier inside the closed car.
- Fuel tank always above half. In the cold, diesel needs anti-gel additive/winter diesel.
- Do not drain the starter battery with lights and charging. Ideally a second battery (dual battery) or a power station.
- Lock the doors, keys in a fixed place (e.g. always the same pocket), head torch next to you.
## Inside the sleeping bag I put
- Next day's clothes (warm in the morning).
- The water bottle (so it does not freeze) and the phone/powerbank (cold drains batteries).
- A bottle for peeing at night (marked!) – you do not go out into the cold.
## In the morning
1. Clear snow from the roof, windows, lights, exhaust BEFORE starting the engine.
2. Put away the panels, air out the cabin.
3. Warm up the engine a little, drive gently for the first kilometres.
## If you must move by car (evacuation, snow)
- Leave in daylight, tell someone your route, follow the authorities' instructions and the main roads.
- Snow chains INSIDE the car and you know how to fit them (try once at home).
- Car kit (see list), water, food, a sleeping bag for each passenger – you may be stuck for hours.
- In bad weather: slow, long distances between cars, no sudden braking. If you cannot see, stop in a safe place.
- Never drive into flowing water or into wildfire smoke.

- Whistle: carries much farther than your voice and does not tire you.
- Mirror / CD / phone: flashes toward an aircraft or ship. Aim with your fingers in a V shape.

- One arm up, one down = “I do not need help”.

- ··· ––– ··· (3 short, 3 long, 3 short).
+ The full Morse alphabet (Greek and Latin) and sending words with light and sound: 🔦 SOS → 📡 Morse.

@@ wild|w_navi|🌞|Navigation without a compass|Sun, shadow, stars
## The sun
- It rises roughly in the EAST and sets roughly in the WEST. At midday (in Greece) it is in the SOUTH.
## Stick and shadow (very reliable)
1. Push a straight stick into the ground. Put a pebble at the tip of the shadow.
2. Wait 15–20 minutes. Put a second pebble at the new tip.
3. The line from the 1st to the 2nd points WEST → EAST.
4. Stand with the 1st on your left and the 2nd on your right: you are facing NORTH.
## Analogue watch
- Point the hour hand at the sun. Halfway between the hour hand and 12 (1 in summer, due to daylight saving time) points SOUTH.
## Polaris (night)
- Find the Big Dipper (the “saucepan”). The two stars at its end point to Polaris, ~5 times the distance between them. Polaris = NORTH.
## Signs that are NOT reliable
! Moss “on the north side” and ants are not reliable. Do not trust them.
+ In the app's “Compass”: hold the phone flat, away from metal and speakers. If it is wrong, move it in “figure eights” in the air to calibrate.

@@ wild|w_food|🍞|Food in the wild|The last thing to worry about
- A healthy person can live weeks without food. Without water, do not eat (digestion needs water).
- First eat what you carry, in portions. Spread the calories – half a portion for many days.
## Safer sources
- Fish (well cooked). Fishing line and hooks fit in a small bag.
- Shellfish from clean waters: limpets, clams – beware of enclosed bays and after rain.
- Snails: ONLY well purged (starved for days) and well boiled.
- Known fruit you are 100% sure of: blackberries, figs, strawberry-tree fruit, chestnuts, walnuts, almonds (sweet), carob.
! NEVER unknown plants, berries, mushrooms. See “Mushrooms & wild plants”.
! Bitter almonds and many fruit pits contain cyanide.

@@ wild|w_mush|🍄|Mushrooms & wild plants: why NOT|The most common deadly trap
! In a survival situation we DO NOT eat wild mushrooms. Never based on a photo, an app or “it looks like”.
## Why
- The DEATH CAP (Amanita phalloides) and its relatives grow in Greece too and look like edible ones.
- Symptoms are delayed 6–24 hours. When they appear, the liver is already damaged. A “false recovery” may follow.
- Cooking, vinegar, the “silver spoon” test, “animals ate it” do NOT work. They are myths.
- Mushrooms give very few calories. The risk is huge, the gain is zero.
## If someone ate a wild mushroom and feels unwell (or you are not sure)
1. Poison Centre 210 7793777 or 166 IMMEDIATELY – even if they feel fine.
2. Keep the remaining mushrooms (and leftovers) for identification.
3. Note the time they were eaten and who ate them. Everyone to hospital.
## Wild plants
- Many poisonous plants look like edible ones (e.g. hemlock like wild carrot/fennel, oleander, thorn apple).
- Eat only greens/fruit you already know 100%, the way grandma picked them, and away from roads and sprayed fields.
+ As a hobby: learn mushrooms with a mycological society and on outings with experts. Not from apps.

@@ wild|w_hyg|🧼|Hygiene and toilet|Disease kills after the disaster
- Wash hands before eating and after the toilet. ALWAYS.
- Toilet in the wild: hole 15–20 cm deep, 60+ m away from water and shelter, cover it.
- Toilet at home with no water: bucket with a double bag, a little bleach or ash/sawdust after each use, lid, keep it outside.
- Rubbish: in closed bags, away from the house (rats).

- Teeth: brush, even with salt/baking soda.

@@ wild|w_weather|🌦️|The weather talks to you|Signs without a forecast
- Clouds building up high like towers in the afternoon = thunderstorm, lightning.
- Halo (ring) around the sun/moon = moisture coming, often rain in 12–24 hours.
- Sudden temperature drop and wind change = a front.

@@ wild|w_after|🔄|After 72 hours|Organising for weeks
## First
- Inventory: how much water, food, medicine, fuel do we have? For how many days?
- Rations: a consumption plan. Perishable fresh food first.
- Shifts: who fetches water, who cooks, who keeps watch, who looks after children/elderly people.
## Community
- Organise with your neighbours: shared kitchen, a list of who needs what, who knows what (doctor, electrician, handyman).
- Find out where the aid distribution points and the municipality's shelters are.
## Safety
- Do not show off your supplies. Lock up.
- Move in groups. Say where you are going and when you will be back.
- Beware of scams (fake “officials”, fake fundraisers).
## Health
- Clean water, hand washing, toilet far away – you stop epidemics.
- Small wounds: clean them daily (they get infected easily).
- Chronic illnesses: find a pharmacy/doctor early, before the medicine runs out.
## Returning home
- Only when the authorities say so. Check for cracks, gas, electricity, water. Photograph damage for compensation before you clean up.
