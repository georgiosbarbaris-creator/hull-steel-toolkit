@@ gear|g_sleep|🛏️|Blanket or sleeping bag?|What keeps you warmer
## Short answer
- For SLEEPING in the cold: sleeping bag + sleeping pad. Much more warmth per kilo.
- For HOME, CAR, FIRE: wool blanket. Tough, does not catch fire easily, stays warm even when wet.
- For the BACKPACK: emergency (foil) blanket + sleeping bag if it fits.
## Comparison
- Wool blanket: + warm when wet, hard to burn, lasts for years, many uses (sling, stretcher). − heavy (2–3 kg), bulky.
- Fleece blanket: + light, cheap, dries fast. − melts in fire, less warmth.
- Cotton blanket: − gives no warmth at all when wet. Not for survival.
- Foil blanket (mylar): + 60 grams, blocks wind, keeps body heat, signalling. − tears, traps moisture inside. Emergencies only.
- Bivy / survival bag (like a mylar sack): better than a foil blanket, ~100–300 g.
## Sleeping bag: what to look for
- COMFORT temperature (ISO 23537 / EN 13537): you sleep comfortably at this. LIMIT means "survive while cold". Ignore EXTREME.
- Southern Greece / islands, winter: comfort about 0 °C to −5 °C. Summer: +10 °C.
- NORTHERN Greece, mountains, winter months (and a home with no power): comfort −15 °C to −20 °C. An unheated house in Florina or Metsovo drops below zero within a few days.
- Better too warm than too cold: if you are warm, open the zip. If you are cold, there is nothing you can do.
- Down: light, packs small, warm – but gives no warmth when wet. Fill power 600+.
- Synthetic: heavier and bulkier, but stays warm when damp. Better for home/car/humidity.
- A "mummy" shape keeps more heat. It needs a hood with a drawcord.
- Extra: a liner (inner bag) adds +2 to +5 °C.
## Head outside
- Keep your nose and mouth OUTSIDE the sleeping bag. Breath inside = moisture = wet filling = cold. Tighten the hood around your face and wear a beanie.
## Foil blanket (emergency blanket): one for everyone
- 60 grams, smaller than a wallet, reflects body heat back. One for each family member in the backpack, one in the car, one in the jacket.
- Use: under the sleeping pad, or wrapped OUTSIDE the sleeping bag/blanket. Inside the sleeping bag only for 1 emergency night (it traps moisture).
- In a shipwreck/for an injured person: wrap the whole body and head, leave the face free.
## SLEEPING PAD: the most forgotten item
- The ground "sucks" the heat out of you. Without insulation you get cold even in the best sleeping bag.
- R-value: summer 2, winter 4+, snow 5+. Two pads together = values add up.
- Foam: cannot puncture, cheap. Inflatable: comfortable, small, but needs a repair kit.
+ Sleep in DRY clothes and a beanie. Eat something fatty before bed. Pee before you sleep – the body wastes no heat on extra fluid.

@@ gear|g_swag|🛌|Swag, bivy and one-person tents|A bed-shelter for one person
- SWAG (Australia): waterproof canvas with a built-in foam mattress and a small "tent" on top. Opens in 1 minute, you put your sleeping bag inside. Ideal for overlanding, a car roof, and also INSIDE the house when there is no heating.
- BIVY (bivvy bag): a waterproof bag that goes over the sleeping bag. Blocks wind, adds 3–5 °C, light (300–700 g). Some have a hoop over the head like a mini tent.
- Survival bag (emergency bivy, silver): 100–300 g, for the backpack and the car. Better than a plain foil blanket because it closes around you.
## The combination that works in the cold
1. Insulation underneath (foam + inflatable, or the swag mattress).
2. Sleeping bag comfort −15 to −20 °C for northern Greece/mountains in winter.
3. Liner inside (+2 to +5 °C).
4. Bivy or swag outside (wind, moisture, +3–5 °C).
5. Foil blanket under everything, or on top in very cold weather.
6. Beanie, wool socks, face outside.
+ All of this also works inside a cold house: a swag or a small tent in the living room makes a huge difference.

@@ gear|g_tent|⛺|Tent – shelter|Do I need a tent?
- For a disaster in the city: usually NO. Shelters, relatives, the car. But a tent helps if you cannot stay in a house after an earthquake.
- For outside the city/mountains: yes, or a tarp + rope, which is lighter.
## What to look for in a tent
- 3-season for Greece. 4-season only for snow/mountains.
- Pitched in 5 minutes by 1 person. Try it at home first.
- Hydrostatic head: fly 2000+ mm, floor 3000+ mm.
- Capacity: get 1 person bigger (2 people → 3-person tent) for gear.
- Family tent: can become a "room" in the garden after an earthquake.
## Tarp
- 3×3 m with grommets, 300–600 g. A thousand ways to pitch it. Together with rope and pegs.
- Cheap option: thick builder's plastic sheet + rope.
+ Always pitch higher than water, NEVER in a dry stream bed (flood) and NEVER under a dead branch.

@@ gear|g_light|🔦|Torches and lighting|What to get
- A HEADLAMP for each member: hands free. The most useful light.
- LED lantern for the room/table.
- Sturdy handheld torch, waterproof IPX4 or better IPX7.
## What to look for
- Batteries: prefer AA or AAA – found everywhere. Or rechargeable via USB-C + spares.
- Lumens: 200–400 are enough for everything. 3000 lumens drain the battery quickly.
- A low mode (5–20 lm) that lasts 50+ hours – this is what you use most of the time.
- Red light: does not ruin night vision, does not wake others.
- SOS/strobe mode.
- Lithium AA batteries: handle cold and 10–20 years of storage. Alkaline ones can leak – take them out of torches in storage.
## Candles?
- Only on a stable base, away from curtains, never while sleeping, never with children alone. Prefer LED.
+ Strap a headlamp onto a water bottle with the light facing the bottle = a lantern for the whole room.

@@ gear|g_power|🔋|Power – charging|Power bank, solar, generator
- Power bank 10,000 mAh ≈ 2 phone charges. 20,000 mAh ≈ 4. Recharge them every 3 months.
- Foldable solar panel 20–30 W: charges a phone during the day (only in sun).
- Power station (large battery 300–1000 Wh): light, router, medical devices, charging. Safe inside the house. With a solar panel = independence.
- Petrol generator: lots of power but noise, fuel, and ONLY outdoors (carbon monoxide).
- USB car charger.
+ Phone: low power mode, no data/Bluetooth, dim screen, no video. Airplane mode when you don't need signal. GPS still works in airplane mode on most phones.

@@ gear|g_solar|☀️|Solar panels: correct use|Power from the sun when everything is down
+ A solar panel with a battery (power station or powerbank) is the only power source that does not run out. In a crisis lasting many days it is vital.
## The two numbers you need to know
- W (Watt) = how “strong” something is RIGHT NOW. 100 W panel, 5 W lamp.
- Wh (watt-hours) = how much energy is stored or used. 500 Wh battery. 5 W lamp for 10 hours = 50 Wh.
- Like a tap and a tank: W is how far the tap is open, Wh is how much water is in the tank.
## How much it really produces
- In practice a panel gives about 50–70% of the W written on it. 100 W panel ≈ 60–70 W in good sun.
- Greece: ~3 hours of “strong sun” in winter, ~6–7 in summer.
- Example: 100 W panel in winter ≈ 100 × 3 × 0.7 ≈ 200 Wh per day. In summer ≈ 450 Wh.
- Cloud: drops to 10–25%. Shade: almost zero.
## What can I power with 200 Wh a day
- Mobile phone: ~15 Wh per full charge → 10+ phones.
- 5 W LED lamp: 40 hours of light.
- Radio: practically all day.
- 10 W internet router: ~20 hours (if the line works).
- Laptop: 3–4 charges.
! Fridge, electric heater, air conditioner, kettle, hair dryer: NOT with a small system. A 2000 W kettle empties a 500 Wh battery in 15 minutes.
## Correct use – step by step
1. Put the panel OUTSIDE. Behind glass it loses 30–60% (modern insulated glazing even more).
2. Point it SOUTH (use the app’s compass).
3. Tilt: in WINTER almost upright (~55° from the ground). In SUMMER flatter (~25°). Simple test: the panel’s shadow behind it should be as small as possible.
4. Turn it towards the sun every 2–3 hours: morning east, midday south, afternoon west. You gain 20–40%.
5. NO shade at all – not even a branch or a railing. A little shade on one part drops the WHOLE panel.
6. Clean off dust, snow, leaves with a cloth.
7. Leave air space behind it: too much heat lowers output.
## The big secret: charge a BATTERY, not the phone
- By day: panel → powerbank or power station. At night: battery → phone, light, radio.
- When a cloud passes, the phone stops and restarts charging and gets worn out. The battery in between protects it.
- Start charging in the MORNING. The day’s sun does not come back.
## What to buy
[ ] Foldable 60–100 W panel with USB-C (PD) and DC output – for backpack and car
[ ] 300–1000 Wh power station (LiFePO4 battery: safer, thousands of cycles) with solar input (MPPT)
[ ] Match the PLUGS before the crisis (MC4, Anderson, DC8020, USB-C). Test the whole system once
[ ] Small 10–20 W panel + 20,000 mAh powerbank for each backpack
[ ] Short cables (long cable = lost energy)
## Safety
! Lithium batteries: not in a hot car in summer, not covered while charging, not next to a child’s bed. If they swell, smell or smoke: take them outside and move away.
- Foldable panels can take drizzle, but the sockets and the box on the back CANNOT. Bring them in when it rains.
- Medical devices (CPAP, oxygen concentrator): measure NOW how many W they draw and how many hours you need. These get first priority for power.
- The car is also a generator: it charges USB with the engine running – ONLY outdoors, never in a closed garage.

@@ gear|g_radio|📻|Radio|The only news when everything goes down
- FM + AM (medium wave) at least. Hand crank + solar + AA batteries = 3 ways to charge.
- Useful: a torch and a USB output for the phone (a little charge).

@@ gear|g_cook|🍳|Cooking – camping stoves|Hot food without power
- Camping stove with butane canisters: cheap, easy. In the cold (below ~0 °C) butane works poorly – get an isobutane/propane mix.
- Alcohol stove: simple, silent, no parts to break. Slow.
- Wood stove (small, foldable): fuel from nature, for outdoors.
- Charcoal grill: ONLY outdoors.
! No flame stove inside a closed space or tent. Carbon monoxide and fire.
## Cookware
- Stainless steel or titanium pot with a lid (the lid makes boiling 2 times faster).
- Stainless steel mug that can go on the fire (for boiling water).
- Spork (spoon-fork), knife, small sponge, lighter.
- Family: 2–3 L pot, frying pan, wooden spoon.
- Thermos: keeps water hot – less fuel.
+ "Haybox" cooking: boil rice/beans 5 minutes, wrap the pot in blankets for 1–2 hours. It cooks with little fuel.

@@ gear|g_water|💧|Water: filters and treatment|What really cleans it
- Hollow-fibre filter (0.1–0.2 μm): stops bacteria and parasites. Does NOT stop viruses and chemicals.
- Purifier (0.02 μm or with chemical): also viruses. For cities after a disaster, prefer this or filter + chlorine.
- Chlorine dioxide tablets: kill almost everything, wait 30 minutes to 4 hours (see instructions).
- Boiling: the surest against germs. Does not remove chemicals.
- Activated carbon filter: improves taste, removes some chemicals, not germs.
- Gravity filter (hanging bucket/bag): ideal for a family.
+ Best combination: pre-filter with cloth → filter → chlorine or boiling.

@@ gear|g_tools|🔪|Knife – tools|The essentials
- Fixed-blade knife 10–12 cm, stainless or carbon steel. For wood, fire, food.
- Multitool with pliers: for repairs.
- Folding saw: clean wood cutting, better and safer than an axe.

@@ gear|g_fire|🔥|Fire: lighters and tinder|3 ways, always
- BIC lighter or similar: the most reliable. Get 3.
- Waterproof / windproof matches (storm matches): burn even in wind.
- Ferro rod: thousands of sparks, works even when wet.
- Ready tinder: cotton with petroleum jelly in a film canister/zip bag, fire starter cubes, pine cone pieces with resin.

@@ gear|g_clothes|🧥|Clothes – footwear|Layering system
1. Base (next to skin): merino wool or synthetic that wicks sweat. NO cotton.
2. Insulation: fleece or down/synthetic jacket.
3. Outer: waterproof windproof shell with hood (Gore-Tex or similar).
- Remove a layer BEFORE you sweat. Sweat in the cold = hypothermia.
! "Cotton kills" in the cold: when wet, it loses warmth and does not dry.
## Footwear
- Hiking boots, broken in, with a good sole. Near fire: leather, not synthetic mesh.
- Wool socks + one spare pair always dry in a small bag.
- Beanie: the cheapest way to get warm.

@@ gear|g_masks|😷|Masks – protection|What protects from what
- FFP2: dust, smoke (particles), viruses. For earthquake, fire.
- FFP3: more protection against particles, radioactive dust.
- Respirator with filters (half/full face) A2B2E2K2-P3: gases and vapours – needs training, a fit without a beard.
! No particle mask protects against gases or carbon monoxide.
- Closed safety goggles for ash and smoke.

@@ gear|g_nav|🗺️|Map – compass – GPS|Don't rely only on your phone
- Paper map of your area and your escape routes.
- Baseplate compass – cheap and needs no battery.
- On the phone: download offline maps in advance (Google Maps offline, Organic Maps, Maps.me). GPS works without internet.
- In the app: the 🧭 “Compass” shows coordinates and guides you back to your saved points without internet.

@@ gear|g_store|📦|Storage – rotation|So nothing goes bad
- Bottled water: in a dark cool place, not next to chemicals. Replace it as the date says.
- Food: "first in, first out". Eat from the stock and refill.
- Batteries out of devices. Power bank charging every 3 months.
- Medicines: check expiry dates every 6 months.
- Backpack: change clothes by season (March & September).
+ Set a reminder in your phone calendar for 1 March and 1 September: “Survival kit check”.
